# Housing and Demographics in U.S. Metro Areas

## Introduction

This project seeks to examine the distribution of housing, income, poverty, and demographics in U.S. metro areas through the use of U.S. Census Bureau's data on Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS ASEC).

Specifically, this study will assess how household and family compositions vary geographically and what role the population size plays in explaining the differences between the metro areas.

## Research Questions

- How do the households in terms of their incomes vary geographically?
- What demographic differences exist across the U.S. metro areas?
- How do the metro areas and the household composition vary in terms of poverty rate?
- How does the data quality and missing values influence the result interpretation?

## Data

The analysis makes use of the CPS ASEC public use data from the year 2024 as well as the state FIPS codes lookup file.

### Data Sources

- U.S. Census Bureau – Current Population Survey, 2024 Annual Social and Economic Supplement
- U.S. Census Bureau – State FIPS/ANSI Codes

## Methods

The project involves:

- Data cleaning and preparation
- Exploratory data analysis
- Geographical analysis
- Income and poverty analysis
- Family and household level analysis
- Data visualisation
- Missing data analysis
- Imputation value analysis

## Key Findings

Differences in income and poverty levels were identified in metropolitan regions and by household type. Differences in poverty rates between Los Angeles and New York are used to assess the difference in poverty rates among single individuals.

The study also considers the effect of missing and imputed data. Medical premium values in the data analysed at the family level were largely imputed.
